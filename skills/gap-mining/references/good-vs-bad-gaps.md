# Good vs bad gaps

Use this to label each candidate in step 5. Give one label and one reason.

## Where gaps are stated

- Discussion: "limitations", "strengths and limitations", "future research", "further studies are needed", "remains unclear", "evidence is limited/inconsistent".
- End of the introduction: "to date, no study has", "little is known about".
- Conclusions of systematic reviews and meta-analyses: low certainty of evidence (GRADE), high heterogeneity, few studies in a subgroup.

## Good gap

A candidate is **good** when most of these hold:

- **Named more than once.** Two or more independent reviews point to it.
- **Live.** The field disagrees about it (conflicting findings, inconsistent results, low-certainty evidence), rather than simply never having looked.
- **Not already being closed.** No registered trial is answering the same question, and no recent preprint or paper already reports it.
- **Studiable.** Data exists (open or controlled on Synapse) or could realistically be collected, or there are enough primary studies to review.
- **Matters.** Answering it would change a clinical, policy or scientific decision, not just add a data point.

## Bad gap

A candidate is **bad** when any of these hold. Name which one in the reason:

- **Geographic reshuffle.** "Not studied in my country/population" with no reason the answer would differ there.
- **Already answered.** A recent paper, preprint or completed trial reports it.
- **Mentioned in passing.** One line in one review, not developed, not echoed anywhere else.
- **Generic boilerplate.** "Larger, longer, better-designed studies are needed" with nothing specific about what is missing.
- **Out of reach.** Needs data, equipment, follow-up time or ethics approval clearly beyond a student project, with no open dataset to stand in.
- **Being answered now.** An ongoing registered trial asks the same question and will report first.

## Beginner filter

The plugin is mainly for people starting from scratch. When two good candidates are otherwise equal, these make one easier to start on. Use them to order the good candidates, and say so:

1. Open data exists on Synapse (no lab, no approval wait).
2. Several reviews name it, so there is enough to read into.
3. The question can be stated without heavy jargon (it will pass the grandmother rule later).

## Labelling rules

- Base the label on what the connectors returned, never on your general sense of the field.
- If the evidence for a label is thin (abstract only, one source), still label it, and add "from abstract only" to the reason.
- Never upgrade a bad gap to good because it sounds interesting. Interest is the user's call.
