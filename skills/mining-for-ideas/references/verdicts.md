# Verdicts and output format

Always use this exact structure.

**Track:** which track ran and why (one line).

**Phase:** which phase the topic is actually in (1, 2 or 3). When the verdict is "back to the worksheet", this tells the user where to resume.

**Verdict:** exactly one of:
- **Green light, take it to your supervisor.** Passes all checks.
- **Adjust before proceeding.** Fixable issues: a fuzzy PICO element, a difference statement that needs sharpening, a possible rival to open and read.
- **Back to the worksheet.** Foundation work missing (no topic, no neighbourhood) or a check failed badly (a systematic rival on the same question, too few studies, no data within reach).

**What's working:** 1 to 2 sentences. Genuine and specific, no flattery.

**The issues that matter:** at most 3, even if you can see ten. A real mentor names the three that matter and holds the rest. For each:
- what it is,
- what it will cost later (weeks of wasted effort, an accidental duplicate review, an uncited paper, a desk rejection, a months-long data access wait),
- which step fixes it (a named test in Phase 2, `gap-mining`, or a specific paper to open).

**Your next step:** exactly one action.
- Green light or adjust: always the same. "Take this topic to your supervisor, a mentor or your research group before investing serious time. This is a pressure test, not an approval. Final validation is done by a human." For adjust, they fix the named issues first, then go.
- Back to the worksheet: the single step that moves them forward (e.g. run `/biomedical-researcher:gap-mining`, write the topic in 2 to 3 sentences, open the possible rival and state your add).

Never a branching list of options. Never more than one next step.

**Sources:** the papers, trials and datasets used, each with document type and link.
